 Investors might feel like they're drowning in data and earnings and today won't throw them a life jacket. Instead, it began with the September 
Personal Consumption Expenditures
 (PCE) price index that came in just as Wall Street expected at 3.6% on an annual basis and 3.1% for core excluding food and energy.
"This is still elevated and too high for the Fed's liking," said Cooper Howard, director of fixed income research and strategy at the Schwab Center for Financial Research (SCFR). "For the Fed, I think it means that December is very much in play. The economy is strong, the labor market is resilient, and inflation is high, which all point to the Fed needing to tighten further."
The 
Federal Reserve's
10-2 vote to pause rates
 yesterday drew little market reaction as stocks finished mostly higher, led by tech. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh declined to hint where rates are headed but noted it's an "information rich" environment—meaning data and other events could shape the next move. Tech crumbled early today after a dramatic miss from 
Alphabet
 (GOOGL), where heavy capital expenditures took a large bite out of free cash flow. 
Microsoft's
 (MSFT) results impressed and 
Amazon
 (AMZN) reports later.

Three things to watch
GDP/PCE deeper dive:
 In addition to PCE, investors eyed the government's first estimate of third quarter 
gross domestic product
 (GDP). "GDP was the bright spot, up 3.4% vs. 3.2% expectations, driven mostly by personal consumption," Howard said. Though consumption rose on a nominal basis, spending softened in real terms, coming in flat. This suggests consumers are spending more for the same or less output. GDP, calculated on a seasonally adjusted annual basis, rose from 2.2% in the second quarter. The GDP chain deflator, which measures price increases across the economy for domestically produced goods, rose 2.2%, less than the 3.5% Briefing.com consensus and far below the 6.1% second-quarter increase. Digging in more, PCE rose 0.5% and core PCE rose 0.3% monthly, both meeting analysts' estimates. Core growth was the same as in August, but headline rose from August's 0.3%, while annual numbers rose from August's 3.4% and 3%. Energy prices drove headline PCE growth, no surprise considering crude's strength. On an annual basis, goods PCE rose 3.9% year over year and services rose 3.5%. Under the surface, about 48% of the subcomponents of PCE are still rising at 3% or more.
Alphabet and Microsoft earnings assessed: Microsoft
 topped analysts' expectations for both earnings per share and revenue. Diving deeper, cloud revenue rose 30%, topping the Street's estimate, while Azure revenue surged 49%, well above expectations. Free cash flow fell year- over-year but easily surpassed Wall Street's estimates. Shares gained nearly 1.5% before the open but felt pressure from overall tech weakness after initially jumping 4%. It was a tough quarter for 
Alphabet
 as earnings per share of $2.12 fell well short of the $3.01 consensus and Search revenue and YouTube ad revenue both missed estimates. The miss on Search—which came as more queries were directed to "AI mode," Alphabet said—raised concerns about AI hurting Alphabet's key product. Google Cloud revenue of $30.7 billion rose 103% annually, beating estimates. Results—which included negative 
free cash flow
 exceeding $9 billion and a prediction that capital expenditures would rise again next year—appeared to make investors question Alphabet's return on invested capital, while Microsoft's quarter triggered positive thinking on that metric. Alphabet's 11% plunge reverberated through the AI sector early today as the 
Nasdaq-100
®
 (NDX) fell more than 1%.
